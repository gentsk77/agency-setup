# P08: Law is append-only; supersede, never edit

## Statement

Adopted rulings and rules are never rewritten or deleted. A change is a new numbered entry that supersedes the old one, plus a one-line status stamp on the superseded entry pointing at its successor, applied in the same change. An agent that finds reality contradicting standing law flags it; only the maintainer retires law.

## Rationale

A decision log is trustworthy exactly insofar as its history is. Editing an old ruling in place destroys the ability to understand why past work looks the way it does, and silently changes what earlier citations of that ruling meant. Supersession preserves the chain of reasoning at trivial cost, and the status stamp makes sure no reader acts on dead law without knowing it. The flag-not-fix rule keeps agents from "helpfully" rewriting law to match their local read of reality.

## What breaks without it

Silent edits turn the log into a palimpsest no one can cite safely, and disagreements about what was ruled become unresolvable because the evidence was overwritten. Readers of un-stamped superseded entries build on retired law in good faith. Both failure modes were observed before the stamp discipline existed.
